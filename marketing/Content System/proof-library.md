# Proof Library

Every number, claim, and citation UpSpeech is allowed to put in public, with the
source attached and the limits written down.

Read this before writing any factual sentence. If a draft needs a figure that is
not in this file, the figure does not exist yet. Restructure the post around a
mechanism instead, or go and find a source and add an entry here first.

**Never invent a number. Never round a number up. Never drop the limit that
travels with it.**

---

## How an entry works

Each entry carries six things:

- **The claim**, in one line.
- **Allowed wording**, which is the sentence you can actually paste.
- **Source**, named and linkable.
- **What it does not prove**, which is the part that keeps us honest.
- **Review**, the date it should be re-checked.
- **Channels**, where it may be used.

## Our own numbers

### The pilot

**Claim.** 24 adults used UpSpeech in a pilot. 21 of them practiced between
sessions. 340 practice recordings were submitted. No push notifications were sent
at any point during the pilot.

**Allowed wording.**

> In our pilot, 21 of 24 adults kept practicing between sessions with no
> notifications from us at all, and submitted 340 recordings. This measures
> engagement, not clinical outcome.

**Source.** Internal, the UpSpeech pilot. Not published, not peer reviewed, and
we say so whenever it appears in front of clinicians.

**What it does not prove.** Nothing about speech. Not fluency, not technique
quality, not therapy outcome, not whether anyone got what they came for. It is an
adherence and engagement signal from a small, self-selected group with no control
arm.

**The tag travels with the number.** "This measures engagement, not clinical
outcome" appears in the same post, the same carousel, the same paragraph. Not in a
footnote, not on the last slide only. If the format has no room for the tag, the
format is wrong for this number.

**Review.** Every quarter, or immediately if a second cohort runs.

**Channels.** All three. It is the strongest thing we have.

### Prior therapy and practice volume

**Claim.** Within that same pilot, patients with no prior therapy experience did
roughly 2.5 times the practice and submitted roughly 5 times the recordings of
patients who had been in therapy before.

**Allowed wording.**

> In our pilot, the people who had never been in therapy before practiced about
> two and a half times as much as the people who had. Small numbers, one cohort,
> and we do not know why yet.

**Source.** Internal, the same UpSpeech pilot. Not published, not peer reviewed.

**What it does not prove.** Causation, and nothing about outcomes. The honest
reading is that it is interesting and unexplained. Several ordinary explanations
fit it, including novelty and prior discouragement.

**Review.** Quarterly.

**Channels.** LinkedIn and the blog, where there is room for the caveat. It is
too easy to misread as a boast on Instagram.

### Product status

**Claim.** The MVP was completed in March 2026.

**Allowed wording.** "We finished the first complete version in March 2026."

**Source.** Internal, the product roadmap.

**What it does not prove.** That the product is finished, tested at scale, or in
wide clinical use. An MVP date is a build milestone, not a maturity claim, and it
never appears as evidence of anything else.

**Note.** The root `CLAUDE.md` and the live `SKILL.md` both say December 2025.
They are wrong. See the divergence table in [`README.md`](README.md).

**Review.** When the next milestone lands.

**Channels.** All three.

### The founder

**Claim.** The founder stutters, and built a team with the skills to change how
people reach speech therapy and how care continues between sessions.

**Allowed wording.**

> UpSpeech was started by someone who stutters, with a team built around the
> problem: how people reach speech therapy, and what happens in the week after
> they leave the room.

**Source.** Internal. The founder's own account of it, which is the only source
this claim can have and the only one it needs.

**A note on phrasing.** The internal north star is bigger than that sentence, and
it should be. The public phrasing avoids "revolutionize" and its family because
house style rules them out, and because a clinician reading "we will revolutionize
speech therapy" from a company with one pilot stops reading. The ambition survives
the rewrite. The credibility does not survive the original.

**What it does not prove.** Lived experience is authority about a life, not about
a clinical field. It never stands in for evidence, and it never leads a post aimed
at a clinician.

**Channels.** All three, sparingly. It is a founding fact, not a weekly one.

## Field numbers, sourced

### Stuttering

**Claim.** About 5% of children go through a period of stuttering lasting six
months or longer. Roughly three quarters of them recover by late childhood,
leaving about 1% of the population who stutter long term. Prevalence in adults is
a little under 1%.

**Allowed wording.**

> About 5 in every 100 children go through a period of stuttering that lasts six
> months or more. Most of them stop. Around 1 in 100 people stutter long term.

**Source.** [The Stuttering Foundation, research on incidence and
prevalence](https://www.stutteringhelp.org/research-incidence-and-prevalence-stuttering);
[ASHA Practice Portal, Fluency
Disorders](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/fluency-disorders/).
The 0.72% adult prevalence figure is Craig et al., 2002.

**What it does not prove.** Nothing about who needs therapy or who benefits from
it. Recovery rates are population statistics and say nothing about an individual
child, which matters a great deal when a parent is reading.

**Careful with this one.** "Most children who stutter recover" is true and is
also the sentence a worried parent misreads as "wait and see". Whenever the
recovery figure appears in parent-facing content, the referral guidance appears
with it.

**Review.** Annually.

**Channels.** All three.

### Speech and language therapists in Europe

**Claim.** ESLA, the European Speech and Language Therapy Association, has 37
member associations in 34 countries, representing over 40,000 SLT professionals.

**Allowed wording.**

> ESLA, the European body for the profession, represents over 40,000 speech and
> language therapists across 34 countries.

**Source.** [ESLA, About Us](https://eslaeurope.eu/about-us/), checked 26 August
2026.

**What it does not prove.** It is not the size of the profession in Europe, and
it is not a market. It is the count of professionals inside associations that
belong to ESLA. Some secondary sources say 50,000 for the same body. We cite the
primary source and the number it publishes.

**Review.** Annually, on the ESLA site itself.

**Channels.** LinkedIn and the blog.

### Developmental Language Disorder

The strongest single figure in this file: population based, peer reviewed, and
immediately legible to a parent or a teacher.

**Claim.** 7.58% of children aged 5 to 6 meet criteria for Developmental Language
Disorder, which the authors describe as roughly two children in every Year 1
class. Counting language disorders associated with another condition as well,
9.92% start school with a language disorder.

**Allowed wording.**

> Around 2 children in every classroom of 30 start school with Developmental
> Language Disorder.

**Source.** Norbury et al., 2016, the SCALES population study.
[Journal of Child Psychology and
Psychiatry](https://acamh.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcpp.12573);
[SCALES final report,
RCSLT](https://www.rcslt.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Final-Report-Surrey-Communication-and-Language-in-Education-Study.pdf).

**What it does not prove.** It is a UK cohort. Prevalence estimates move with the
criteria used, and this study says so itself. It says nothing about UpSpeech,
which does not serve DLD today.

**Status.** Stocked for the next condition focus, not for today. Any post using it
is education about the field, not a product post.

**Review.** Annually.

**Channels.** Blog and LinkedIn.

### Speech sound disorders

**Claim.** 3.6% of 8-year-olds have a persistent speech sound disorder.

**Allowed wording.**

> Around 4 in every 100 children still have a speech sound disorder at age 8.

**Source.** Wren et al., population cohort, [Journal of Speech, Language, and
Hearing Research](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2015_JSLHR-S-14-0282).

**What it does not prove.** Preschool prevalence estimates for speech sound
disorders range enormously depending on the criteria applied, which is why we
cite the persistent figure at a fixed age and never a range.

**Status.** Stocked, same as DLD.

**Channels.** Blog and LinkedIn.

## To verify before first use

**The WHO access figure.** Widely repeated: around a billion people worldwide
could benefit from speech and language therapy, and roughly one in ten reaches a
qualified provider. It is the single clearest statement of why UpSpeech exists.

It currently only appears in secondary sources, so it is **not usable yet**. Trace
it to a WHO primary document, add the entry here with the document named, and it
becomes usable. If it does not trace, it does not get used, however good it sounds.

## Never public

These are not "handle with care". They do not appear, in any channel, in any
phrasing, including in a reply to a comment.

### Pricing

No prices, no tiers, no ranges, no "from", no currency at all. Pricing is not set.

When somebody asks what it costs, the answer is that pricing depends on how a
clinic works and we would rather have the conversation than quote a number that
turns out to be wrong. Templates are in
[`community-management.md`](community-management.md).

### Regulatory and medical-device framing

UpSpeech is not positioned as a medical device, and we do not write sentences that
put us in the category. The rule is broader than the obvious words, because the
category is entered by implication.

Never write: MDR, medical device, Class I, Class IIa, self-certified, CE marked
for a clinical purpose, clinical-grade, certified, validated, regulated,
diagnostic, assessment tool, or anything that describes the product as detecting,
measuring, assessing, screening, or evaluating a person's speech.

The live `../CONTENT_DIRECTIVES.md` claim table still names MDR Class I. It is
out of date. See [`README.md`](README.md).

Safe alternatives are all descriptions of mechanism: it records, it shows, it
tracks, it drafts, it reminds, it makes the week visible. The clinician decides.

### Unverified market sizing

The 12,017-SLPs-in-Spain figure that appears in the live `SKILL.md` has no source
we can find. It is retired. Market sizing in general only appears when it traces
to a named primary source, and when in doubt we leave the size out. A post that
needs a market size to be interesting is usually an investor deck in disguise.

### Investor material

ARR, revenue projections, funding, valuation, runway, growth targets. None of it
is public content, in any form, including "we are growing fast".

## Sourcing hierarchy

When a post needs a fact we do not have, sources rank in this order.

1. Peer-reviewed research, cited by author and year.
2. Professional bodies: ASHA, ESLA, RCSLT, and the national associations
   (APTF in Portugal, AELFA in Spain, NVLF in the Netherlands).
3. The Stuttering Foundation and established community organizations.
4. National statistics offices, for population figures only.

**Never a source:** another company's content-marketing blog, an SEO statistics
roundup page, an AI-generated summary, or a figure whose only trail leads to
another blog citing a blog. Most "speech therapy statistics 2026" pages are the
fourth kind. If the trail does not end at a named study or a named organization,
the number does not exist.

## Adding an entry

1. Find the primary source. Open it. Read the actual figure in context.
2. Write the entry with all six fields, including what it does not prove.
3. Record the date you checked it.
4. If you cannot write the "what it does not prove" line, you do not understand
   the number well enough to publish it yet.
