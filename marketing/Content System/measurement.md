# Measurement

What we track, and when we actually look at it.

**The stance, from `../blog/strategy.md`: traffic is not the metric. Being quoted
is.** UpSpeech is selling into a profession that decides by trust, slowly, in
conversation. Reach that produces no trust is a number that feels good and means
nothing.

---

## Per channel

### Instagram

**Primary.** Saves, and shares to stories or DMs. Both mean somebody thought this
was worth keeping or worth showing another person, which is the only Instagram
signal that correlates with anything we care about.

**Secondary.** Replies to story prompts. Profile clicks. DMs from people who
stutter or from parents.

**Ignore.** Follower count on its own. Likes. Reach on a post that nobody saved.

**The one qualitative signal that outranks the numbers.** What people say in the
DMs. A single message from a person who stutters saying a post described their
week accurately is worth more than a month of reach, and it belongs in the review
verbatim.

### LinkedIn

**Primary.** Substantive comments from actual SLPs, meaning a comment with an
opinion in it rather than a reaction. Reshares by clinicians and educators. Saves
on document carousels.

**Secondary.** Profile-to-site clicks. Connection requests from clinicians. DMs
from clinic owners.

**Ignore.** Impressions. Likes from founders and other tools in the category,
which is most of the early engagement on a B2B account and none of the value.

**The signal that matters most.** A clinician sharing a post with a colleague,
unprompted. Hard to measure, easy to notice, and worth recording by hand when it
happens.

### Blog

**Primary.** Being cited by an answer engine on a non-branded prompt. Ranking for
the exact question the post was written to answer.

**Secondary.** Organic sessions per post. Time on page for the long ones.
Technique-page clicks from articles.

**Ignore.** Total pageviews. One viral post about nothing is not progress.

## AEO, quarterly

The one that matters most over a year, and the one easiest to forget because
nothing prompts it.

`../aeo.md` records the baseline: **zero mentions across ChatGPT, Gemini,
Perplexity, Copilot, and Google AI Overviews on non-branded prompts.** UpSpeech
ranks first for its own name and is invisible for everything else.

**Every quarter, re-run the same prompts from `../aeo.md`, unchanged**, and record
share of voice per engine. Changing the prompts destroys the comparison, which is
the whole point of a baseline.

Moving off zero on a single buyer prompt is a bigger result than a year of
Instagram growth, because it compounds and because it reaches people at the moment
they are deciding.

Two preconditions from `../blog/strategy.md` cost more than any article and are
worth checking on every quarterly run: the prerender failure on the technique
pages, and the missing `/for-slps` and `/for-people-who-stutter` landing pages.

## The monthly review

Half an hour, on a fixed day. Filled in from
[`content-log.md`](content-log.md).

```text
MONTH:

Published:        n posts (ig / li / blog)
Against plan:     [on / behind, and why]

Top 3:            what, and the actual reason it worked
Bottom 3:         what, and the actual reason it did not

Best comment:     verbatim
Best DM:          verbatim, anonymized

Learned:          one sentence that changes what we do next month
Changed:          what we are editing in the directives because of it

Next month:       one goal, one experiment
```

**"The actual reason it worked"** is the only line that matters. "Good hook" is
not a reason. "It named the specific Tuesday-evening moment and three people said
so in the comments" is a reason, and it is reusable.

---

## Experiments

One at a time. Two at once and you learn nothing from either.

```text
EXPERIMENT:
Hypothesis:   [what we think and why]
Change:       [the one thing that differs]
Sample:       [how many posts, over what period]
Measure:      [the single metric that settles it]
Result:       [what happened]
Verdict:      [keep / drop / inconclusive]
Directive:    [what changed in the docs, or nothing]
```

An experiment that changes no document was not an experiment, it was a mood. The
verdict lands in `../CONTENT_DIRECTIVES.md` or in
[`post-formats.md`](post-formats.md), or it is forgotten by March.

Worth running early: hook style on LinkedIn (number first vs observation first),
carousel length on Instagram (four slides vs seven), whether the honest-limits tag
helps or hurts engagement on a stat card, and posting time.

---

## What we do not measure

**Vanity totals.** Followers, impressions, likes, as headline numbers.

**Anything about individual patients.** Not usage, not progress, not practice
volume, not as content and not as a metric in a public deck. Real usage data
becomes content only in aggregate, and only through
[`proof-library.md`](proof-library.md) with a source, a limit, and an entry.

**Competitor social performance.** We do not name competitors and we do not
benchmark against their post counts. It produces posts written at another company
rather than at a reader.
