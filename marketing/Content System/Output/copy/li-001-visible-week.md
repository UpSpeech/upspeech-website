## Post text

```text
We built the reminder system, then turned it off before the pilot started.

21 of 24 adults practiced between sessions anyway, and submitted 340 recordings.
We wanted to know what adherence looked like without pressure, and there was some
real nervousness about the answer.

What seems to have carried it is that the practice was short, tied to the
technique the therapist had actually set, and visible to that therapist
afterwards. Practice that somebody is going to look at behaves differently from
practice that disappears.

This measures engagement, not clinical outcome. The clinical evidence work is a
separate and much slower project. But it changed what we build: the effort goes
into making the week visible rather than into getting better at nagging.

If you set home practice, what actually gets it done in your caseload?

#SpeechTherapy #SLP #Stuttering
```

Hook truncated at 140 characters reads: *"We built the reminder system, then turned
it off before the pilot started. 21 of 24 adults practiced between sessions anyway,"*
