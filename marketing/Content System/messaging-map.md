## The one-liner

> **Speech therapy that continues after the session.**

PT-PT: **Terapia da fala que continua depois da sessão.**

It works because it names the actual gap without naming a condition, which keeps
it true as the product widens past stuttering. It does not promise an outcome, it
does not mention AI, and a clinician can read it without wincing.

Second position, when the reader is specifically clinical:

> **The week between sessions, made visible.**

PT-PT: **A semana entre sessões, finalmente visível.**

## Value proposition by reader

Personas are defined in [`audiences.md`](audiences.md).

| Reader | What we say | What we never say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Person with a communication difficulty | Five calm minutes, on the technique your therapist set, wherever you are. Someone is going to see it. | That it will make you fluent, or that fluency is the point |
| Parent or caregiver | A clear picture of what your child is working on, and a way to support it without becoming their therapist | Anything about falling behind, or fixing |
| Speech and language therapist | Home practice you can actually see, and a session report that arrives as a draft | That it saves you N hours, or that it assesses anything |
| Clinic | Continuity of care across a caseload, and less of the evening spent on documentation | A price, a promise about capacity, or vagueness about data |
| Wider community | Stutter-positive education with nothing attached to it | Anything at all with something attached to it |

## The three things we are allowed to be proud of

Each one is a mechanism, not a result. Mechanisms are checkable, and they are more
convincing to a clinician than a result we cannot support.

**1. Practice that somebody is going to look at behaves differently.** Practice
tied to the technique the therapist actually set, and visible to that therapist
afterwards. In our pilot, 21 of 24 adults kept practicing with zero notifications
sent. That measures engagement, not clinical outcome, and we say so in the same
breath.

**2. The report arrives as a draft.** The clinician reads it, changes what is
wrong, and approves. The judgment stays theirs. The typing mostly does not.

**3. Stutter-positive by design, not as a tagline.** The product never scores a
person on how fluent they sounded. That is a design decision visible in the
software, which is a stronger claim than a value statement on a website.

## Objection handling

The answer column is the actual wording to use, including in comments and DMs.

**"Is this a medical device?"**

> No. UpSpeech is not a medical device and is not positioned as one. It does not
> diagnose, assess, or measure anyone's speech. It records practice, shows it to
> the therapist, and drafts the paperwork. Clinical decisions stay with the
> clinician.

**"Does the AI assess or score speech?"**

> No. The AI drafts a session report from what happened, and the therapist edits
> and approves it. It does not judge anyone's speech and it does not produce a
> clinical opinion.

**"Where is the evidence?"**

> Early and honest: one pilot, 24 adults, no control arm, not published. It shows
> that people kept practicing without being nagged. It shows nothing about
> outcomes, and clinical evidence is a separate and much slower project we have
> not finished.

**"Does it replace the therapist?"**

> The opposite. Everything it does is either something a therapist asked for or
> something a therapist approves. Its whole value depends on there being a
> therapist at the other end of it.

**"What happens to patient data?"**

> Each clinic's data is isolated from every other clinic's. Nothing is used to
> advertise to anyone and nothing leaves the clinic's tenant. Detailed answers go
> to the clinic in writing rather than in a comment thread.

**"Why not just a homework sheet?"**

> Homework sheets work fine right up to the point where nobody knows whether the
> homework happened. The difference is not the exercise, it is that the week is
> visible to both people before the next session starts.

**"What does it cost?"**

> Pricing is not set yet, and it will depend on how a clinic actually works. We
> would rather have that conversation than quote a number that turns out to be
> wrong for you. See [`community-management.md`](community-management.md) for the
> full reply and the PT-PT version.

**"Is this only for stuttering?"**

> Today, yes. Stuttering is where we started, and the gap between the session and
> the rest of the week is wider there than almost anywhere. The product is built
> to go further, and we will say so when it does rather than before.

## Product vocabulary

Capitalize product surfaces. Do not capitalize ordinary nouns.

| Write | Not |
| --- | --- |
| Learning Path | learning journey, curriculum, program |
| Journey (only as the name of the product screen) | using "journey" as a metaphor anywhere else |
| Companion | mascot, avatar, assistant, bot |
| Scenarios | simulations, role-play module |
| session report | note, documentation output, SOAP note |
| practice | homework, exercises, tasks, drills |
| recording | submission, upload, sample |
| technique | skill, method, strategy |
| the week between sessions | the gap, the interval, downtime |

`../CONTENT_DIRECTIVES.md` bans "journey" as a metaphor. The exception is narrow:
the product screen called Journey, referred to as itself.

## What UpSpeech is not

Useful when a draft is drifting.

- Not a therapy replacement, and not therapy.
- Not a diagnostic or assessment tool.
- Not a fluency trainer. It does not have an opinion about how smooth anyone
  sounded.
- Not a consumer wellness app that happens to mention speech.
- Not a medical device, and not seeking to be described as one.
- Not a documentation product with a patient feature stapled on, and not a patient
  app with a clinician dashboard stapled on. The two halves are the point.
