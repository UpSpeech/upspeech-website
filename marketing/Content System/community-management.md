# Community Management

How we reply, and what to do when a reply is hard.

This is the highest-risk surface we have. A post gets reviewed before it ships. A
comment reply is written in thirty seconds by whoever saw it first, in a space
where somebody may have just told us something difficult about their life.

Read this before the first hard comment arrives.

---

## The default

**Reply to everything substantive, the same day.** On LinkedIn the comment thread
is the distribution. On Instagram, with this audience, replying is the whole trust
argument.

**Tone.** The same voice as the posts, slightly warmer and much shorter. Calm,
plain, on their side. Read it aloud before sending.

**Length.** Two or three sentences. A paragraph if they asked something real.

**Never in a reply:** a claim we would not put in a post, a price, a diagnosis, a
recommendation about anyone's treatment, or a product pitch answering a question
that was not about the product.

**Emoji.** Instagram, sparingly, where a person would use one. Never on LinkedIn.

---

## The hard situations

### 1. Someone tells us they are having a bad time

The most common hard comment, and it usually arrives under an encouraging post.
Somebody says the thing they have not said out loud.

**What we do.** Acknowledge it. Do not fix it, do not advise, do not sell.
Something warm and short, and then let it be.

> That sounds like a heavy week. Thank you for saying it here. There are a lot of
> people reading this who will recognize it.

PT-PT:

> Parece uma semana pesada. Obrigado por o dizeres aqui. Há muita gente a ler isto
> que se vai rever no que escreveste.

**If it goes further than that,** meaning any mention of self-harm, crisis, or
someone in real distress: do not handle it in a comment thread. Reply once, warmly,
pointing to real help, and escalate to the founder the same hour.

> I am sorry, that sounds really hard, and it is more than a comment thread can
> hold. Please talk to your doctor, or to a support line. In Portugal, SOS Voz
> Amiga is 213 544 545, daily from 15:30 to 00:30.

**Only ever name a line from an official list, and check it the day you use it.**
The Portuguese health service publishes the current crisis lines at
[saudemental.min-saude.pt/linhas-de-crise](https://saudemental.min-saude.pt/linhas-de-crise/),
and SOS Voz Amiga publishes its own numbers and hours at
[sosvozamiga.org](https://www.sosvozamiga.org/). Numbers and opening hours change.
Never improvise a helpline, and never give a line that is closed at the moment
somebody needs it.

### 2. Someone asks for clinical advice

"My son does this, is it stuttering?" "Should I be doing easy onset for this?"

**We never answer the clinical question.** Not a soft version, not a hedged
version. We are not their clinician, we have not assessed anything, and a wrong
answer here does real harm.

> I am not able to answer that one, because it really does depend on things only
> someone who has met you can see. A speech and language therapist can tell you in
> one appointment. If it helps, here is what we generally write about when people
> ask when to go: [link]

PT-PT:

> Isso não te consigo responder, porque depende mesmo de coisas que só quem te
> avalia consegue ver. Um terapeuta da fala consegue dizer-te numa consulta.

**Redirecting is not a brush-off if the redirect is useful.** Point at a technique
page, an article, or the plain "asking early is not the same as something being
wrong" line.

### 3. Criticism from the stuttering community

Somebody says a post framed fluency as the goal, used the wrong word, or sounded
like every other company in this space.

**Do not defend.** The instinct to explain what we really meant is the wrong one
in public and it makes the second comment worse than the first.

**If they are right**, say so, fix the copy, and say that you fixed it.

> You are right, and that is on us. We have changed the caption. Thank you for
> saying it rather than scrolling past.

**If it is a genuine disagreement** rather than an error, one reply stating our
position plainly, then stop. We do not argue with the community we are trying to
serve.

**Then act.** A fair criticism goes into the next review, and if it points at a
rule we are missing, it goes into `../CONTENT_DIRECTIVES.md` or
[`brand-signature.md`](brand-signature.md) so it cannot happen twice.

### 4. A parent asking whether this will fix their child

The most tempting question we get, and the easiest one to answer badly.

> It will not, and nothing will, because stuttering is not a thing that gets
> fixed. What it does is make the practice their therapist set easier to keep up
> during the week, and it lets the therapist see how that week actually went.
> Whether that is useful for your child is a question for their therapist.

PT-PT:

> Não vai, e nada vai, porque a gaguez não é uma coisa que se corrija. O que a
> UpSpeech faz é ajudar a manter, durante a semana, o treino que o terapeuta
> definiu, e permitir que o terapeuta veja como correu essa semana.

That answer loses a small number of prospects and keeps every clinician who is
reading the thread.

### 5. A clinician challenging the evidence

The best comment we get. Answer it fully and honestly.

> Fair question. What we have is one pilot: 24 adults, 21 practiced between
> sessions with no notifications from us, 340 recordings. No control arm, not
> published, and it measures engagement rather than any clinical outcome. Proper
> clinical evidence is a separate and much slower piece of work that we have not
> done. I would rather say that than dress the pilot up as more than it is.

Never inflate the pilot. A clinician who catches an overclaim in a comment tells
colleagues, and they are right to.

### 6. Someone asks what it costs

Pricing is not set, and the never-public rule in
[`proof-library.md`](proof-library.md) means no number, no range, no "from".

> Pricing is not fixed yet, and honestly it depends a lot on how a clinic works.
> Send me a message and I will walk you through where we are.

PT-PT:

> Ainda não temos preço fechado, e sinceramente depende muito de como a clínica
> trabalha. Manda-me mensagem e explico-te onde estamos.

### 7. Somebody offers their story

Generous, and the answer is a slow yes.

> That means a lot, thank you. If you would be up for us sharing any of it, let me
> send you how we handle that first, because we do not put anyone's story out
> without written consent and a say over the wording.

Then the consent process below. Never in the comment thread.

## Direct messages

**Same rules, plus three.**

1. **No clinical advice, ever.** The privacy of a DM makes it feel more acceptable
   to give. It is not.
2. **No patient data in a DM.** If a clinician starts describing a real patient,
   move it to a proper channel before they get further.
3. **No screenshots of anyone's app.** Not ours to share, in either direction.

---

## Consent

**Nothing involving a real person ships without written consent on file.** Rule 5
in `../brand-voice.md`, and the one that would be most damaging to break.

Covers: a patient, a clinician, a clinic name, a real session, a quote, a
recording, a screenshot with real data, a photograph.

### The process

1. Ask privately, never in a comment thread.
2. Send what we would publish, in full, in the exact wording.
3. Get written agreement back, naming the channel and the wording.
4. Record it in the register.
5. Send them the post when it goes live.
6. Honor a withdrawal immediately, without asking why.

### Register fields

Held by the founder, outside this repo, wherever the consent forms live.

```text
person:             name
role:               patient / clinician / clinic
what:               quote, photo, recording, screenshot, clinic name
exact wording:      the text as approved
channels:           which ones, specifically
date given:
expires:            a date, or none
withdrawn:          date, if ever
where published:    permalinks
```

**Consent is specific.** Consent for LinkedIn is not consent for Instagram.
Consent for a quote is not consent for a photograph. Consent given in 2026 for one
campaign is not consent for a reuse in 2027.

---

## Escalate to the founder

Same hour, not same week:

- Anyone in distress or crisis.
- Any mention of harm.
- A legal, regulatory, or data-protection question.
- A journalist.
- Criticism gathering momentum rather than sitting still.
- Anyone asking us to remove something about them.
- A clinician reporting that a patient was upset by something we published.

When unsure, escalate. Nobody has ever regretted flagging a comment that turned
out to be fine.
