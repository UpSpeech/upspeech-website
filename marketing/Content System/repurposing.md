# Repurposing

One idea, three channels, without repeating yourself.

The rule underneath everything here: **never repost, always rewrite.** The same
idea reaches a clinician and a person who stutters through different doors, and
copy that tries both doors at once opens neither.

---

## The source unit is the article

Write the blog post first, when there is going to be one. Long form is where the
thinking happens, and the social posts are extractions from it. Going the other
way produces articles that are three captions stapled together, and it shows.

When there is no article, the LinkedIn post is the source unit and the Instagram
posts are extractions from that.

---

## The matrix

One article, worked through the channels over about three weeks.

| Output | What it takes from the article | Format | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blog article | All of it | BL-1 or BL-2 | Week 1 |
| LinkedIn document carousel | The argument, as seven pages | LI-4 | Week 1, two days later |
| LinkedIn text post | One idea from it, rewritten as prose | LI-1 or LI-3 | Week 2 |
| Instagram carousel | The most concrete section | IG-8 | Week 2 |
| Instagram statement | The one line worth keeping | IG-2 | Week 3 |
| Story frames | The FAQ questions, one per frame | IG-10 or IG-12 | Any week |
| Newsletter, when there is one | The article, with a personal opening | | Week 3 |

Format codes are in [`post-formats.md`](post-formats.md).

## The rewrite rules

**Change the reader, and the post changes completely.** Not the tone, the whole
thing: the opening, the evidence chosen, the ending, the length. An idea rewritten
for a clinician cites the limits; the same idea for a person who stutters names a
Tuesday evening.

Worked example, one fact, two channels, in `../CONTENT_DIRECTIVES.md` at the end.
Read it before doing this for the first time.

**Never post an article's text as a LinkedIn post with a link out.** LinkedIn
suppresses external links and the post reads like an RSS feed. Write a real post
about one idea from the article, and put the link in the first comment or in a
follow-up.

**Never repost an Instagram caption to LinkedIn.** Same idea, rewritten for a
clinician, or skip it.

**One number can carry three posts.** The pilot numbers work as a LinkedIn
observation, an Instagram encouragement, and a blog direct-answer paragraph,
because each frames the same fact for a different reader. The engagement tag
travels with it in all three.

---

## The reuse window

**Six weeks** before an idea can come back around, on any channel.

An idea can be posted again after that, rewritten, if it earned its place the
first time. [`content-log.md`](content-log.md) is what makes this checkable, which
is the main reason the log exists.

The exceptions that reset the clock:

- The evidence changed.
- A feature shipped that changes the answer.
- It is tied to a date, like International Stuttering Awareness Day.

---

## Refreshing rather than repurposing

Blog posts decay differently from social. A social post is finished in 48 hours. A
blog post is an asset that goes stale.

**Review every article at six months.** Update the figures, add anything new we
have learned, refresh the visible updated date, and re-check that the direct-answer
paragraph still answers the question the way people are now asking it.

A refreshed article usually beats a new one on the same subject, and it does not
split the internal linking between two pages competing for the same question.

---

## What never gets repurposed

**A consented story stays on the channel it was consented for.** Consent is
specific, always. See [`community-management.md`](community-management.md).

**A parent-facing post does not become a clinician-facing post.** The reframing
that would be needed is large enough that it is a new post, and doing it lazily is
how a reassuring post turns into something that reads as marketing to a
professional.

**An education post does not become a product post.** The stutter-positive pillar
carries nothing to sell. Reusing an education post with a product line added
breaks the contract that pillar runs on, and the community notices.

**Anything that failed.** Repurposing a post that did not work spreads the
failure. Work out why it failed first.
